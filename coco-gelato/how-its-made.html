The method

Gelato, not ice cream

They are not the same dessert. Gelato has less butterfat, is churned slower so it takes in less air, and is kept a few degrees warmer. The result is denser, colder on the tongue for less time, and the flavour hits harder. The words on this page are a placeholder for the demo.

A recipe from Italy, a hand from Japan

The base recipes are Italian: milk-forward, low fat, balanced for sugar and solids so they scoop at the right temperature. The chef trained in Japan, where the same care goes into matcha, black sesame and yuzu. That is why the tea and nut flavours here taste like the ingredient and not like sugar.

Everything is made in small batches on site. A tub that runs out is not refilled from a store room; it is churned again, usually the next morning.

From milk to cone

1 · Balance

Milk, cream, sugars and the flavour base are weighed to a recipe. Get the ratio wrong and it freezes too hard or melts too fast.

2 · Age

The mix rests cold overnight so the fats and proteins settle. It churns smoother the next day.

3 · Churn

Slow paddles, low air. A batch is small, a few litres, so nothing sits for long.

4 · Blast & hold

Frozen hard fast to keep the crystals tiny, then held at serving temperature in the case.
